Overview

Controllable video generation is increasingly expected to function as a world model, yet existing benchmarks largely measure whether a model reproduces explicitly specified targets. They do not fully reveal whether the model understands the initial scene and generates the consequences that an instruction leaves unstated.
We introduce WorldExam, a hierarchical diagnostic benchmark that evaluate world models together with 4 diagnostic levels, namely: visual quality, control adherence, spatial consistency, and world reactivity.

Visual Quality

measures the quality of the video itself, including perceptual plausibility, temporal stability, and visible artifacts

Control Adherence

measures whether the controlled camera or subject moves as instructed.

Spatial Consistency

measures whether the model maintains a coherent world, especially when the camera revisits a previously observed viewpoint.

World Reactivity

evaluates whether a model can infer and generate the scene-conditioned reactions or valid execution paths left unstated by the input.

We instantiate this hierarchy through eight dedicated tasks: Camera Control, Subject Control, Scene Revisit, Terrain Interaction, Object Interaction, Social Interaction, Physical Reaction, and Goal Completion. Together with six visual-quality metrics, these tasks yield a total of 14 evaluation metrics, forming two complementary scoring systems—static-scene evaluation and dynamic-interaction evaluation—that assess distinct aspects of world-model capability.

Key Findings

  • Strong visual quality, control adherence, and spatial consistency do not imply that a model can infer unstated reactions or execution paths; conversely, success on World Reactivity tasks does not compensate for visual artifacts, control errors, or spatial drift.
  • Existing video world models exhibit a clear capability divide. Camera-driven models excel at precise camera control and scene revisiting; action-driven models provide stronger direct subject control; and language-driven models perform better in semantic interactions and high-level goal completion, although they remain less precise in composed and fine-grained control.
  • Instruction following does not necessarily imply world reactivity. A model’s ability to execute explicitly specified targets does not mean that it can infer unstated execution processes or the resulting consequences.
  • World reactivity remains a major bottleneck for current models. Although many models can control a subject’s actions, terrain, objects, nearby agents, and physical processes often fail to respond appropriately, indicating a limited understanding and simulation of dynamic environments.
